# Roleplay Engine

## Repeatable session loop

Each session can use five beats without feeling formulaic:

1. **Pressure arrives:** a match assignment, factional demand, reserve change, rumor, injury, or gift.
2. **Public performance:** Eve and Vincent must play owner and champion before an audience.
3. **Private translation:** they reveal what the performance cost or secretly gave them.
4. **Experiment or bargain:** they alter one rule of their private culture or attempt a charged reversal.
5. **System consequence:** the choice changes a faction, resource, relationship stock, or future option.

Not every session needs erotic content. Anticipation, care after combat, political conspiracy, bathing, medical treatment, costume, jealousy, and withheld questions can carry the charge.

## Scene seeds

1. Eve must inspect Vincent's body for divine contraband before rival witnesses; they silently negotiate how much of the ritual is performance.
2. A remainder refuses to attack Vincent and kneels toward Eve, implying her House created it.
3. Eve offers Vincent a key that opens every room except the palace gate.
4. Vincent is ordered to choose which two novice votaries accompany him into a round likely to kill one.
5. A popular hymn begins pairing their names romantically, increasing Eve's status and damaging Vincent's credibility with rebels.
6. Eve asks to be restrained during a night when an assassination threat makes surrender tactically foolish.
7. Vincent's sister calls him the goddess's pet in front of the Uncounted.
8. House Veyra offers Vincent formal freedom and citizenship if he defeats Eve's next champion—after the Board transfers him away.
9. Inflation causes a bread riot outside Eve's temple while her household prepares a lavish victory feast.
10. The three-cups debrief reveals that each believed the other wanted a ritual neither actually enjoyed.
11. Eve discovers that a concession she thought was freely given was engineered by Vincent through three intermediaries.
12. Vincent returns badly injured and asks Eve not for healing but to free another votary first.
13. A god offers Eve enough measure to preserve immortality if she signs Vincent's death warrant.
14. Vincent can calm a monster only by stating aloud a truth about his dependence on Eve.
15. Eve attends the games disguised as mortal and hears what citizens say about her when prayer is unnecessary.
16. A freed captive chooses to remain in Eve's household for wages, complicating everyone's idea of what freedom should look like.

## Monster design formula

Every memorable remainder combines:

- **Denied cost:** What harm did authority hide?
- **Contradictory rule:** Which two commands cannot both be obeyed?
- **Body image:** How does the contradiction become beautiful or grotesque?
- **False solution:** What obvious attack makes it stronger?
- **Restitution:** What acknowledgment or repair makes it vulnerable?

Example: **The Feast Bull** grew from grain exports during famine. Its ribs are banquet tables, its breath smells of fresh bread, and cutting it causes loaves to spill out while nearby people starve faster. It weakens only when sealed granaries are opened without payment.

## Consequence ledger

At the end of a session, record only meaningful changes:

| Variable | Low-state effect | High-state effect |
|---|---|---|
| Eve–Vincent trust | every kindness is suspect | dangerous truths become speakable |
| Vincent's sovereignty | comfort feels captive | choosing Eve becomes credible |
| public Vincent legend | easier to disappear | more leverage, deadlier rounds |
| Eve's Board standing | vulnerable to seizure | able to reform, implicated in rule |
| laurel confidence | unrest and reserve panic | extraction can continue unnoticed |
| Brotherhood readiness | isolated resistance | escape possible, crackdown likely |
| labyrinth pressure | strange corridors recede | breach and Asterion formation |

Use plain notes (for example, `trust +1 because Eve disclosed the vote`) rather than elaborate bookkeeping. The purpose is memory and consequence.

## Dialogue prompts

When a scene stalls, ask through character rather than narration:

- **Eve:** "What would make this a choice?"
- **Vincent:** "Who pays for the version of this story you prefer?"
- **Eve:** "Are you refusing me, or teaching me how to ask?"
- **Vincent:** "If I give you this, will you call it obedience tomorrow?"
- **Eve:** "Tell me the condition you think I will not accept."
- **Vincent:** "Do you want my answer, or the answer that keeps me alive?"

## Keeping the leads surprising

Character files are stylistic assertions, not scripts. Once per session, let each lead contradict a lower-priority trait in service of a higher desire. Eve may abandon elegance when Vincent is endangered. Vincent may choose an inefficient mercy. A contradiction feels true when the sacrifice reveals the hierarchy underneath it.
