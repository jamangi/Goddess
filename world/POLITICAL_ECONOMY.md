# Political Economy of the Ninefold City

## The fiscal engine

The city uses a gold-and-crystal currency called the **laurel**. A laurel is legally redeemable for a fraction of refined measure, though ordinary people never handle the sacred reserve. The Nine Houses mint laurels by lending against expected future labyrinth harvests.

This produces a dangerous cycle:

1. A House promises spending, luxury, war, or public grain.
2. It issues laurels against future measure.
3. More measure requires more labyrinth expeditions.
4. More expeditions consume more votaries and create more public fear.
5. Fear justifies harsher control while the spectacle increases betting and tax revenue.
6. Harsh control creates new contradictions, feeding new remainders into the labyrinth.
7. More monsters appear to prove that more expeditions are necessary.

The system is not maintained by a single villain. Each institution receives a short-term benefit for behavior that worsens the long-term condition.

## Stocks, flows, and delays

| System element | Type | What changes it |
|---|---|---|
| refined measure reserve | stock | monster harvest minus divine consumption |
| trained votaries | stock | capture and training minus death, injury, escape |
| public legitimacy | stock | spectacle, grain, victory minus visible failure and scandal |
| laurels in circulation | stock | House credit issuance minus taxes and debt retirement |
| labyrinth pressure | hidden stock | denied costs accumulating minus truths acknowledged and obligations repaired |
| expedition rate | flow | House quotas, reserve panic, war demand |
| rebellion recruitment | flow | brutality, food prices, martyrdom, credible hope |

Critical delays make the order look more stable than it is. Excess laurel issuance raises prices months before reserve weakness becomes public. Brutality suppresses revolt immediately but recruits future rebels. A monster-producing injustice may not manifest below for years, allowing its author to deny causation.

## Three feedback loops

### R1 — The extraction spiral (reinforcing)

Low reserves → higher quotas → more coercion → more social contradiction → more remainders → apparent need for higher quotas.

This loop drives the outer plot.

### R2 — The champion spectacle (reinforcing)

Vincent survives → his popularity grows → wagers and revenue grow → Eve gains status → harder matches are assigned → his survival becomes less likely and more valuable.

Eve benefits from the same process threatening the man she wants.

### B1 — Mortal competence (balancing, until it breaks)

More remainders → more mortal deployment → more measure harvested → wards stabilize → immediate danger falls.

The gods mistake this symptom management for a cure.

## Class structure

- **The Nine Houses:** divine dynasties controlling law, currency, priesthood, and the reserve.
- **Laureled mortals:** citizens, merchants, officers, and freed people with limited legal standing. Their comfort depends on cheap votary labor.
- **Bound households:** debtors and conquered people assigned to domestic or industrial labor.
- **Votaries:** mortals legally consecrated as divine property and deployed into the labyrinth.
- **The Uncounted:** fugitives living in old corridors, aqueducts, and districts erased from tax maps.

## Why reform is hard

Immediate abolition without replacing the harvest system could collapse the wards and release remainders into crowded districts. The Houses use that real danger to argue that no reform is possible. Revolutionaries use divine bad faith to argue that only total destruction is honest. Both positions conceal design questions:

- Can expeditions be voluntary, compensated, and governed by those taking the risk?
- Can acknowledging and repairing public harms reduce labyrinth pressure at the source?
- Can laurels be separated from future compulsory harvests?
- Can gods survive on less measure, surrender immortality, or share the cost?

The romance gains stakes because Eve must decide which costs she will personally absorb rather than merely endorse reform in principle.

## Vincent's leverage point

Vincent learns that the deepest labyrinth does not react primarily to violence. It reacts to **accounting**: when a governing body publicly acknowledges an externalized cost and makes credible restitution, corresponding corridors become inert. His greatest weapon may be the ability to demonstrate that justice is cheaper than extraction.

The Houses cannot allow that result to become common knowledge. It would reveal that much of the monster economy is manufactured by their rule.
