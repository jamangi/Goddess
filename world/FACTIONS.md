# Factions

## Eve's House of the Open Door

Once a prestigious mediator among the Nine, Eve's House has become dependent on Vincent's wins and the revenue around them. Its emblem is a key passing through a pomegranate. Its servants are better treated than most and therefore used as propaganda for "benevolent bondage."

Internal fracture: old chamberlain Marcell wants Eve safe and solvent; younger votaries see Vincent as proof that the House could become a sanctuary rather than a prettier cage.

## The Board of Nine

The gods coordinate reserves, quotas, public games, and war debt through a formally unanimous council. In practice, decisions emerge from favors and veto threats. The Board knows the reserve is lower than announced. It does not know that the labyrinth is responding to its fiscal policies.

Goal: preserve divine continuity and prevent a run on the laurel.

## The Sand Brotherhood

A secret mutual-aid network among votaries, trainers, healers, and corpse-bearers. It began as a burial fund. It now moves messages, medicine, weapons, and people. Members disagree between negotiated emancipation and a mass breach from the city.

Goal: make survival collective rather than a privilege granted to famous champions.

## The Uncounted

Communities of fugitives occupy disused sections of the upper labyrinth. They know some corridors can be calmed through restitution and ritual truth. They distrust Vincent because celebrity is a form of proximity to power; they distrust Eve for better reasons.

Goal: remain illegible to the city's ledgers and eventually open a route beyond the wards.

## House Veyra, Keepers of Victory

Eve's divine rivals manage armies and most public arenas. They want Vincent transferred into their ownership or killed in a match impressive enough to restore faith in divine supremacy.

Goal: turn the emerging crisis into centralized military rule.

## The Horned Choir

Priests who listen to the labyrinth through bronze resonators. Publicly they interpret monster appearances as moral judgments against mortals. A minority have realized the pattern points upward, toward divine policy.

Goal: control the meaning of the labyrinth. Its reformers want to publish what they hear before orthodoxy silences them.

## Faction pressure rule

No faction should enter merely to deliver lore. It should ask one lead to pay a cost the other cannot ignore:

- a House offers Eve safety in exchange for Vincent's autonomy;
- the Brotherhood asks Vincent to exploit Eve's private trust;
- the Uncounted demand a public act that would destroy Eve's standing;
- the Board offers reform with a delay that will kill people now;
- Veyra offers Vincent freedom if he publicly repudiates Eve.
