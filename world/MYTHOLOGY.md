# Mythology: The Heart of Measure

## The forbidden origin

Before the Ninefold City, the gods appointed **Orun the Measurer** to divide possibility: sea from shore, oath from impulse, life from afterlife, price from appetite. Orun could perceive every exchange and every delayed consequence. The other gods loved his judgments when they constrained mortals and hated them when they constrained gods.

A mortal temple-servant named **Ilyra** discovered that Orun had engineered a famine to make a rebellious valley dependent on divine grain. The public myth says she killed him while he slept, cut out his still-counting heart, and ate it before the gods could inherit his office.

The act did not make her omnipotent. It made her able to see systems: reservoirs behind droughts, incentives behind laws, fear behind obedience, and leverage inside apparent helplessness. She used that sight to break the valley's grain monopoly. The gods burned the valley, but Ilyra's descendants escaped.

Every human now carries a vanishingly small portion of the heart, called the **Red Measure**.

## What the inherited power does

The Red Measure is not generic magic. It expresses as a sensitivity to relationships and flows:

- noticing when a stock is being depleted despite reassuring surface numbers;
- feeling the cost displaced onto an unseen person or future moment;
- recognizing feedback loops, thresholds, and leverage points;
- forming binding reciprocal pacts with people, places, or tools;
- redirecting a limited flow that already exists, never creating power from nothing.

Most mortals experience it as intuition. Training, danger, and close bonds can sharpen it. A master artisan may "feel" a supply chain; a midwife may sense a village's health; a general may perceive morale moving before troops do. Vincent's expression is unusually strong in combat and social systems. He reads the labyrinth as a set of hungers and the arena crowd as a single turbulent organism.

The gift has costs. The user can become overwhelmed by too many connections, mistake a compelling pattern for truth, or optimize a system while forgetting the people inside it. Vincent's great intellectual temptation is to treat Eve as a set of levers.

## Why the gods need humans

Orun's death removed the divine faculty that metabolized contradiction. Unresolved divine promises, abandoned names, unpaid sacrifices, and laws that conflict with one another now accumulate beneath reality. In the labyrinth they acquire flesh as **remainders**: horned, beautiful, grotesque creatures built from costs the gods refused to acknowledge.

Gods are powerful against matter but nearly blind to their own contradictions. A remainder slain directly by a god reforms. A mortal carrying the Red Measure can perceive its governing contradiction and make the killing permanent. The corpse crystallizes into usable **measure**, the substance that maintains divine immortality and the city's wards.

Thus the divine order rests on a humiliating dependency:

> The gods punish mortals for possessing the only power that can clean up divine mistakes.

## The labyrinth

The labyrinth is not merely a building. It is the materialized remainder of Orun's unfinished mind, growing under the city in response to hidden social costs. Corridors reconfigure around denied truths. A district driven into hunger may generate granary chambers with mouths in the walls. A broken treaty may become two mirrored armies that cannot perceive each other.

The famous horned beast, **Asterion**, is not one creature but a recurring office: the labyrinth selects a remainder to become its warden. Killing an Asterion buys years of stability but does not cure the system that produced it.

In other words, every monster is a bill coming due.

## Eve's divinity

Eve governs thresholds, appetite, and chosen bonds—doorways, first tastes, vows entered with open eyes, and the dangerous instant when wanting becomes action. She can open or close physical and social passages, intensify an acknowledged appetite, and sanctify reciprocal agreements.

She cannot create genuine desire. Attempts to force affection corrupt her own domain, weakening her magic and making her physically ill. This is why Vincent's uncommanded attention has both emotional and divine value to her, and why she develops private forms that make his refusals legible.

## The hidden truth

Ilyra confronted Orun with the famine's accounting. He had obeyed the Nine and rationalized the valley's suffering as a stabilizing cost; when forced to measure that choice honestly, he concluded that no immortal—including himself—could be trusted with authority over immortal excess. He willingly offered Ilyra his heart. The transfer nevertheless required her to kill him with her own hand and consume the heart before any god could inherit his office.

Humanity's "original theft" was therefore a violent but consensual transfer of stewardship. The Nine found Orun dead, suppressed the pact, and constructed a punitive theology that concealed both their complicity in the famine and their dependence on humanity.

The hidden truth is an in-world mystery. Early evidence remains fragmentary so competing versions create investigations, factional pressures, and a revelation Eve has strong incentives not to believe.
