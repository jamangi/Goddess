## Operating procedure

1. Preserve the submitted prose and comments verbatim in `posts/Pnnn/source.md`. Record author, date, fragment/full status, preceding context, and provenance. Never silently replace a source with a revision.
2. Assign stable paragraph or beat references. Separate supplied facts, textual observations, interpretations, and unknowns.
3. Write an analysis with anchored measures, continuity checks, response opportunities, and a post-level writer profile. Read the source before looking at prior writer hypotheses to reduce confirmation bias.
4. Update the pattern register with supporting AND contrary examples. Update the writer profile only when the new evidence warrants it. A profile summarizes a conditional range; it need not explain every choice with one personality trait.
5. Provide a mechanical edit and, when useful, a writer-aligned alternative. Explain consequential changes and their tradeoffs. Neither becomes canon or a submitted RP post automatically.
6. Record the author's selection and eventual partner response separately. A revision is a prediction until someone evaluates or uses it. Only independently authored material counts as new writer evidence.

## What a psychological profile means here

A revisable account of **creative priorities, attention allocation, narrative habits, and explicitly shared intentions**. Character motives, narrator claims, and writer beliefs are different layers. Fiction alone does not establish real-life sexual preferences, mental health, trauma, attachment style, or hidden motives. Personal self-reflection can be recorded as the writer's own report, with its scope and date, rather than presented as something the lab discovered.

The useful ambition is to become better at predicting which revision a writer will recognize as theirs. Agreement with a flattering portrait is weaker evidence than correctly predicting a choice between alternatives. Writers can correct, narrow, or retire any interpretation.
